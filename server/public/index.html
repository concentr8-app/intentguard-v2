Ready in three minutes.

  1. Install it

    Open the download and drag Concentr8 into Applications. If macOS says the app is damaged, it is not. Run this once in Terminal:

    xattr -cr /Applications/Concentr8.app

  2. Add your courses

    In the app, choose Add your courses and paste a bCourses access token. No token button? The Calendar Feed link from bCourses works too. You can skip this and type any task.

  3. Turn on the call

    Open Settings, then Morning call. Enter your US number, pick a time, tick the box and choose Start my morning calls.

    Want to hear it now? Choose Call me now.

One short call. Then the Mac helps you keep your word.

You decide the task and the time out loud. That is the part that is usually missing at 9 a.m.

  • 8:00

    The call

    It reads your upcoming bCourses deadlines and asks one question: which task, and when? You answer in a sentence. The call takes under a minute.

  • 9:30

    You start

    The app knows your task and your start time, and your session begins. You can also start any task by typing it in, even one that is not in bCourses.

  • 9:41

    You drift to YouTube

    The app compares what you are doing with the task you chose, so course pages and the work itself count as focus. Known distractions get a visible warning and a short grace period. If you stay, the app moves you back to your work instead of just scolding you.

  • 10:15

    You are stuck

    Press I'm stuck. A calm screen guides one minute of box breathing, then you get one tiny first step for the next five minutes. Still stuck? A smaller step, or a short call to talk it through. After five distractions in ten minutes the calm screen starts by itself, and Esc ends it.

  • 11:00

    You take a break on purpose

    Breaks are allowed. Pause the nudges, end the break when you are ready, and monitoring picks up again.

  • Later

    You see what happened

    Each session keeps a plain log of the nudges and returns, so you can look back without guessing.

Built on research, not hunches.

Every part of Concentr8 goes back to a study about getting started, several of them with people who have ADHD.

  • ½ grade

    College students with ADHD earned about half a grade less in all four years, and fewer stayed enrolled.

    Why we built Concentr8.

    DuPaul et al., 2021 · 406 college students
  • Since 1997

    UC Berkeley researchers have followed girls with ADHD into adulthood. Trouble with school did not simply fade with age.

    Why it checks in every day, not once.

    Hinshaw lab, UC Berkeley · 228 participants
  • 94 studies

    Deciding exactly when you will start, called an if-then plan, clearly helps people follow through.

    So the morning call asks: which task, and when?

    Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006 · meta-analysis
  • If → then

    If-then plans helped children with ADHD hold back unwanted responses, up to the level of children without ADHD.

    The same idea, tested with ADHD.

    Gawrilow & Gollwitzer, 2008
  • 2 in 3

    Adults with ADHD who learned skills like breaking big tasks into small steps: more than 2 in 3 saw symptoms drop by 30% or more, against about 1 in 3 with relaxation training.

    So when you are stuck, you get one tiny first step.

    Safren et al., JAMA 2010 · randomized trial
  • 5 min

    Five minutes a day of structured breathing, box breathing among them, improved mood over four weeks.

    So I'm stuck starts with a minute of box breathing.

    Balban et al., Stanford 2023 · 108 adults

These studies are why we designed Concentr8 this way. Concentr8 itself has not been tested in a study, and it is not a medical product.

Claude does the thinking. You do the starting.

Concentr8 uses Claude, Anthropic's AI, at the few moments where a person would help most. Each one has a plain fallback, so nothing breaks without it.

The conversation

The morning call and the stuck call are conversations with Claude. Cartesia gives it the phone line and the voice.

Your real deadlines

Before each call, Claude writes the first sentence from your bCourses deadlines, so the call starts with what is actually due.

One tiny first step

When you press I'm stuck, Claude turns your task into one step small enough to start in the next five minutes.

What Claude sees

Your task titles, course names and due dates, and what you say on the call. Never your screen, your files or your browsing.

Questions

macOS says the app is damaged. What do I do?

The app is not signed by Apple yet, so macOS blocks it after the download. It is not damaged. Open Terminal (press cmd and space, type Terminal) and run this once:

xattr -cr /Applications/Concentr8.app

Then open Concentr8 from Applications as usual. If macOS only asks whether you want to open it, choose Open.

Windows says it protected my PC. What do I do?

The app is not signed yet, so Windows warns before the first start. Click More info, then Run anyway. Remember that Windows is an early version: calls, tasks and the stuck screen work, blocking distractions does not yet.

How do I start the Linux version?

Make the file runnable once, then double-click it:

chmod +x Concentr8-x64.AppImage

On newer Ubuntu versions you may need the package libfuse2 first. Linux is an early version too, without blocking distractions.

Do I always get the newest version?

Yes. The download buttons always give you the newest release. The installed app does not update itself yet, so to update, download it again and replace the old one.

What does the app need permission for?

Concentr8 asks macOS for Automation and Accessibility access so it can see which app and page you are on and move you back to your work. It asks when it needs them, and you can change this in System Settings.

What do you store about me?

Your bCourses token stays on your Mac. The call server keeps your phone number, your call time and the titles and due dates of your assignments, so the call can name them. Delete your account in the app and this data is removed.

Which phone numbers work?

US numbers only for now. Enter yours with the country code, like +1 415 555 1234.

What if I do not want the call anymore?

Say "stop calling me" during the call, or turn it off or delete your account in the app.

Which computers does it run on?

Macs with Apple Silicon (M1 or newer) and Intel Macs, with everything. Windows and Linux have early versions with the morning call, tasks and the stuck screen, but without blocking distractions; the full versions come later. There is no iPhone or Android app yet.
